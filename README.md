# Compound Days — Waterside

Build **v36**, “A Bench for Evening Gist”, on the authored-character line: Ada’s bench, carried in by two players on one device and sat on together, on top of v35’s three-day script (the v34b living-compound loop tuned into a three-day script with a morning, a veranda evening, a purse that says no, paint for the flat, sound cues and a day log, on the v33 environment and first evening with the authored v21 character model and wardrobe). The exact id is stamped in `dist/index.html` (`BUILD_ID`), shown in the game Menu and recorded in `PACKAGE_MANIFEST.json`.

A browser life game set in a Nigerian compound. Create two independent players, move the sofa into your flat, shop, cook, learn crafts, furnish your home and host neighbours.

## Play

Choose **Start with a companion** to play alone, or **Start a new household on this phone** for two people sharing one screen.

- Player 1: WASD to move, E to interact.
- Player 2: arrow keys to move, Enter to interact.
- Phones: use the on-screen sticks and action buttons. Landscape gives both players more room.
- Saves stay in that browser on that device. Published v30–v33 share an origin and save schema; v33a reads those saves and maps their retired looks (see below).

Cross-device play requires the inherited host's room/database API, which is unavailable on this static hosted copy. Companion and same-screen play are supported.

## v33 — The first evening

New households arrive at 3pm with six saved milestones: move the sofa, gather jollof ingredients, cook together, host neighbours, choose a home gift, and choose tomorrow’s visit. Open **Tasks** on desktop or phone for the journal. The normal free-play activities remain available.

- Existing saves stay unchanged until the player chooses to follow the evening; existing achievements count.
- The guided pre-party clock pauses at 6:30pm so ordinary wandering does not close the market. Pause guidance to restore normal time. There is no real-time completion deadline.
- A one-time optional welcome basket fills only missing jollof ingredients. A meal that was eaten or sold can be cooked again before hosting.
- Action-boundary saves preserve cooking answers and party choices. Continue reopens an unfinished recipe or party.
- The first guided housewarming avoids the rooftop rain detour and always offers a photo until one is taken. Later parties keep their existing weather behavior.
- A plant, rug or wall art reward can be claimed once. A full flat sends it to Build storage. The following morning delivers the player’s chosen Yard invitation.

## v36 — A Bench for Evening Gist

One new module, `dist/src/bench-v36.js`, with its stylesheet `dist/src/bench-v36.css`, loads straight after `lens-v35.js`. It wraps earlier functions and calls them; the base page, the life modules, the bridge and the v35 modules are unchanged. Nothing changes until a delivery is started: no mesh, no collision, no save field, and outside a local pair only one Menu line saying why there is no bench.

What two players can do now:

- **Fetch Ada’s bench.** In a local two-player household, once the sofa is in, from day 2 the Menu opens with “Ada left her bench at your gate.” and the Today page (Tasks, or the Menu’s “Your day in Palm Court”) has a card, “Ada’s bench needs two pairs of hands”. **We’ll fetch it** opens Ada’s note; **Off we go** puts a wooden bench across the street at the gate with a pale ring on the ground at each end. If the crate sits on the way into the passage, somebody pushes it aside (with a toast). If a piece of furniture stands on the bench’s veranda spot or just inside the flat door, the Menu says which piece and where, and the delivery does not start; while a delivery is open, Home and Build will not put a piece there.
- **Carry it together.** Each player stands at a ring and presses their own act key: the first **Takes this end**, the second **Lifts together** (from round a corner, where the short step to the end would pass through a wall, the prompt says **Come round to this end** instead). One player is **Move** (yellow chip) and the other **Turn** (teal chip), shown beside each player’s prompt in their own half of the screen. Move pushes towards or away from their own end and the bench slides along its length; Turn pushes their end left or right and the bench swings round its middle. One player can never move it alone: Move cannot turn it and Turn cannot walk it, so every corner needs both (“small-small, turn your side… okay, come”). The route is the gate, the courtyard, the narrow passage on the left (corner 1), the flat door (corner 2), the room and the veranda doorway.
- **A gentle carry.** It speeds up over 0.4 s and stops almost at once, so a short tap is a small nudge (under 3 cm). Near walls it slows down. A holder squeezes in close to their end (0.22 m, not a walking player’s 0.33 m), so brushing a post or a wall with an elbow no longer stops the bench: the route has twice the room it had (about 0.20 m of slack instead of 0.10 m). When something does stop it, the knock comes with a puff of dust where the contact is (beside the holder when it was their body, at the bench’s end when it was the bench), a short recoil, a pulse on that player’s chip and a line under both prompts (“You’re up against the wall.” / “Efe is up against the wall.”, or “The bench is against the post.”); nothing breaks, and the bench stops at the wall instead of creeping into it. After a few seconds of pushing into the same wall the prompts say “Stuck? Back up a little, or set it down and swap roles.” (in the prompt itself, so phones show it too). A faint arrow appears when a player pushes in a direction their role cannot use. While the bench is held, a faint outline of it shows through anyone or anything standing in front of it.
- **Walls that guide, never turn.** (Round 2, “guided lateral”.) When Move’s push along the bench is stopped by a jamb or a post, the bench steps sideways by a centimetre or two at a time (at most 0.15 m for each contact) and carries on if that frees it; when Turn’s swing is stopped, the bench’s middle steps up to 0.08 m away from what the swinging end met. In the passage, the flat door and the veranda doorway, a bench already within 15° of the opening’s line is drawn sideways towards its middle (0.30 m for each metre pushed). These allowances come back only after 0.30 m of unhelped travel, or a set-down and a lift. None of this ever turns the bench: only the Turn player changes its heading, so every corner is the turner’s job and one player alone still cannot deliver it. The openings’ middles are measured against the walls each time the walk grid is rebuilt, and an opening that does not match switches its pull off. No line announces the help. Because “sideways” follows the bench while the keys follow the camera, the turner gets two small aids: their faint arrow appears after 0.35 s (the mover’s after 0.6 s), and after three refused swings within 4 s the prompts read “Your end is on the wall. Try the other way, or ask Efe to back up a little.” and, for the mover, “Ola needs room. Back up a little.” for 4 s or until the bench has turned 3°. Ada’s note now says “one of you in front to walk it, one behind to turn it”, the lift toast says “Lifted. Efe walks it, Ola turns it.”, and the first-lift hints are “Push it along: towards your end or away from it.” and “Push your end left or right to turn it.” While the bench is lifted the place-name captions (“The Waterfront” and the rest) step out of both views, and exactly those come back afterwards.
- **Set it down, swap, let go, reset.** Either holder’s act key sets it down at any time, and a set-down ends a stall. If that spot would shut somebody in, the bench shifts a little first (“Bit further… there.”) or the game says why not. While one player holds it on the ground, their act key swaps Move and Turn, and walking away lets go. A person does not walk through their partner, so a holder counts as standing in the way: a player whose taking an end would shut the other out of their only way round to the far end is asked to leave it (“Ola needs this end”) and takes the other one, a holder who ends up in the other’s only way round sees **Let go · Ola can’t get round** (the other sees “Ask Efe to let go”) and lets go by themselves after 2.5 s, and a holder stepping back at a set-down or a let-go lands on their own side of any wall. At an end, **Take this end** is never hidden by a room prompt such as **Cook**. Only a bench on the ground that nobody can lift (the crate pushed onto an end, say) offers **Reset the bench** beside it, and never instead of **Take this end** at an end. The Menu, at its top while a delivery is open, always has **Bench stuck? Put it back** (the bench goes back to the furthest place both players can still lift it from, or to the gate) and, for a first delivery, **Leave the bench for now** (Ada keeps it; the offer comes back).
- **Pause and find.** Beside the reset, **Pause delivery and explore** sets a carried bench down with the same safe set-down (it can still say why not) and gives both players their own keys back; in the chooser it leaves the bench in the doorway. While it lies on the ground, **Find the bench** (in the Menu and on the Today card) puts the destination marker and the distance pill on it until either player takes an end. Carrying on is walking back and lifting; lifting it again in the doorway reopens the chooser. The game clock stops while the bench is carried, chosen or gliding home, and runs while it lies untouched, while one player holds an end and while the pair sit, so the evening still comes. A held E or Enter never repeats an Act in the bench (no flip-flopping roles, ticks or set-downs), and when the window loses focus or the tab is hidden every held key and thumb is let go: a carried bench stops where it is, still held.
- **See where you are going.** Each half of the screen keeps both holders, the bench and the next corner in view, and a faint chevron marks the next corner on the ground. On a small phone view the corner point gives way to an edge arrow, which points where that view draws the next corner and sits on the edge of the area the camera frames, clear of the player card and the prompt. On phones the Pantry card steps aside while the bench is held, and after the first two lifts the carry prompt is just the key and the chip. The wayfinding pill steps aside everywhere while the bench is held, being placed or sat on (its “Use Act” would mean something else then).
- **Choose together.** When the bench reaches the veranda doorway (one holder on the veranda, the other in the room) it is set down there and a strip opens in each half: “Where should Ada’s bench go?” with three arrangements of the one veranda spot that fits a 1.5 m bench, west of the pepper pot: **Looking out** (facing straight out over the rail), **Turned to the gate** and **Turned to the water**. A bright ghost shows the choice in place. Either player changes it (A/D or ←/→, or the strip’s ◀ and ▶ buttons); each agrees with their own act key or their own “… agrees” button in their own view (which shows the tick when pressed), and any change clears both ticks. The ghost is drawn in the Move yellow when that arrangement is free (red when it is not), its body as well as its glow, with a broad outline at seat height and a large arrow the way the seat faces, both drawn through the parapet; faint outlines show the other two arrangements, and the arrangement’s name is large in the strip. When both agree, the bench glides from the doorway to its spot; the holder on the veranda first steps aside east of the doorway. When a piece of furniture stands there (the armchair, chairs or a plant on the veranda cell beside the doorway), it goes in two legs: the room holder steps aside, the bench backs into the room, the veranda holder comes in through the doorway, and the bench glides out to its spot. The glide is planned while the pair is choosing, a little each frame, and every frame of it, and every moment between frames, keeps the bench clear of the walls, the pot and both players (if no clear glide exists at all it fades across instead). The camera frames it for a moment. On phones the strip sits at the top of each view, over the player card, so the doorway bench and the ghost stay in sight. The Menu’s **Put it down in the room for now** leaves the chooser.
- **Sit together.** Beside the placed bench, from its east end, **Sit on the bench together** seats both players side by side, facing out over the rail towards the street. Opening the Menu, Tasks or the journal no longer stands them up; moving, **Stand up**, or a new day does. The bench is a Home item (“Wooden bench”) that raises the vibe; Build cannot store it or pick it up.
- **Again.** **Carry Ada’s bench again** in the Menu brings the same bench back to the gate while the placed one waits, hidden, until a new arrangement is agreed (**Leave the bench where it was** cancels and keeps the placed arrangement everywhere, whatever the chooser was showing, and leaves no trace of the cancelled run: in playtest mode the next run is still pre-swapped from the last completed one). Ada thanks the pair once for carrying it in themselves, and her day-six line about the bench follows the delivery.
- **Playtest mode.** `?bench=playtest` (used once, then removed from the address) makes the next delivery start with each player in the job the other started the last one with, adds the carry time, bumps and resets to the toast after each agreement, and adds **Bench runs** to the Menu. `docs/TESTING_bench.md` is the owner’s 25-minute two-player playtest script.

Controls: Player 1 moves with WASD and acts with E; Player 2 moves with the arrow keys and acts with Enter (directions follow the camera). Pads: stick and A. Phones: the two thumb zones and the two Act buttons. In the chooser Player 1 uses A/D and E, Player 2 ←/→ and Enter.

Decisions taken by the owner for this build:

- The three placements are three arrangements of the one veranda spot that fits the bench: facing straight out, angled towards the gate, angled towards the water. No second 1.5 m spot on the veranda row leaves the pot and the walkway reachable.
- The pair sit facing out over the rail towards the street. The courtyard cannot be seen from the veranda.
- The task needs two humans on one device. From day 2 with the sofa in, a companion household’s Menu and Today page say “Ada’s bench needs two pairs of hands” with **Play this with two players on this device**: it switches the household to two-player controls for the delivery and gives the companion back exactly (where it stood, the companion setting and the single view) when the pair stand up from the placed bench, when the day ends with it placed, when the delivery is paused, left or cancelled, or from **Back to my companion**; a reload in between stays two-player with that way back. An online household’s says “Carry Ada’s bench when you are both on one device.”; nothing else appears. Online, a bench that is not yet placed is absent on both devices, only a placed bench’s arrangement is shared, and a pair sitting on the bench stand up. Back on one device, anyone standing where the absent bench lies is moved clear and the bench stays where it was.
- The carry ends in the veranda doorway, and the bench then glides to the confirmed spot (in two legs when a piece stands beside the doorway).

Other behaviour worth knowing: the day-three veranda evening waits while the bench is in someone’s hands or being placed, but not for a bench left lying on the ground (a pair who leave it at the gate still get their evening).

## v35 — Three days in Palm Court

Seven new files load after the bridge, in this order: `dist/src/tune-v35.js`, `dist/data/lines-v35.js`, `dist/src/days-v35.js`, `dist/src/evening-v35.js`, `dist/src/audio-v35.js`, `dist/src/homeuse-v35.js` and `dist/src/lens-v35.js`, plus the stylesheet `dist/src/days-v35.css`. Each wraps earlier functions and calls them; the base page, the four life modules and the bridge are unchanged. Old saves keep working and keep their money, and new save fields are only written when first needed.

What a player can do now:

- **Start small.** A new household starts with ₦6,000 instead of ₦55,000. The welcome basket still fills whatever jollof needs, so the first evening always completes, and the companion still buys what is missing at the market. Old saves keep their purse.
- **Grow one plant over three days.** At the housewarming Tunde promises a seedling. On the second morning it is waiting by the veranda pot with his note, and Today points to it. Plant it and water it; it is taller the next morning, with white flowers at the top. Water it again and the peppers ripen at once, ready to pick: three peppers, shown on their own card in the Garden page (with buttons to find Mama Nkechi and Tunde when they are waiting for them), and the pot starts again. Once the pot needs nothing more that day, its directions marker clears.
- **Fish with one button.** The bite comes 2.2 to 4.4 real seconds after the cast, even on a slow phone. The float twitches just before the bite. While the fishing panel is open, Act (E, Enter or the touch Act button) presses Cast, Reel in, Keep and Cast again, so the whole loop is one button, and Cast again casts straight away. Act never presses anything outside fishing.
- **Save up for something.** Ada’s woven armchair is a day-two find at ₦9,000, more than any first-day purse. A fish for Mama Nkechi brings ₦1,000, and one to six catches sold from the catch card close the gap. Finds never asks you to confirm a purchase; when your companion is nearby it adds one approving line to the purchase toast (one toast, not two). Until it is yours, the armchair's card at Finds says it comes from Ada’s workshop, and on a new household’s first day Ada can be invited to the housewarming without a talk first, so her armchair story is heard there.
- **Sit outside.** The armchair and the chairs may stand on the veranda. Three cells stay clear and show dashed with a one-line legend: the walkway outside the door, the path to the pot, and the room cell just inside the door (the only way out to the veranda), so three veranda cells are free to decorate. A piece may close off the far end of the row: the armchair beside the pot leaves the empty corner beyond it out of reach, and that is allowed. What is never allowed is a placement, move or turn that would leave the pot or the walkway unreachable on foot from inside the flat; that is refused with one line (“That would block the way along the veranda.”) and the piece stays chosen or in hand. The check runs only when a piece is put down or turned, never every frame. Choosing a different stored piece on Home now really chooses it (before, the first choice came back). After placing a piece, “See it in the room” brings it into the camera’s view for a moment.
- **Paint the flat.** Three wall colours (Adire indigo, Palm green, Ochre limewash) and two floors are always on offer at Finds for ₦1,500 to ₦2,500. Once bought, paint the front room from Home as often as you like, with one undo. The colour survives a reload. Adire indigo is now a softer indigo limewash (`#6D8AB8`): the old deep blue was almost the blue tee’s own colour, so a player in it vanished against the wall. The new one is well clear of every top a player can wear; by day it reads chalky, at dusk a dusky slate indigo.
- **Two furnishings that do something.** A placed TV console’s screen glows softly on the lamp’s hours, from six in the evening, until the household sleeps. A placed coffee table carries a round tray with a jug of zobo and three cups while neighbours visit: from the veranda evening’s arrival until the last guest has gone, and through the housewarming. Both follow the piece when it is moved or turned, and need nothing new in the save.
- **Try a higher camera (optional, off by default).** The Menu has a “Camera: classic” / “Camera: high and close” button right after Sound, kept per device. High and close is a 30-degree lens looking down at 50 degrees instead of 37, from the same direction, so the veranda wall no longer hides the armchair and more of the flat shows; the width seen around you is the same, and the carry, fishing and evening framing refit by themselves. The creator, the wardrobe photos and the title camera are unchanged. To try it, tap Menu → “Camera: classic”, or open the game with `?lens=30` added to the address (for example `http://localhost:8000/index.html?lens=30`), which turns it on without saving anything. Faces tilt a little from the higher angle, so judge it on a real phone.
- **Mornings that follow on.** Each morning Today opens with a card about what happened overnight (the seedling, “It grew overnight”, returning finds, Ada’s invitation, fresh shells, an evening-fish hint), with the right neighbour’s request underneath; once that morning’s job is done (planted and watered, or picked) the card says so instead of pointing back at the pot. While the veranda evening is still to come, it leads the morning on alternate days, and on the others the day’s own morning shows with the evening card below it. The rest buttons on Today and the night page read “Sleep until tomorrow”.
- **A veranda evening.** From day three, between six and ten in the evening, while you are in the flat or on the veranda, Tunde, Mama Nkechi and Ada walk in from the gate (on screen for their last steps), stand in a row along the street side of the rail, from just beside you to under two metres west, each turned towards you without showing the camera a back (the speaker in three-quarter), and each say one line about what you have done; Ada’s is about the armchair if it is placed. Your companion walks round through the door to stand beside you (or steps there when the talking starts), and the camera keeps you, your companion when it is within about two metres of you, and all three neighbours in view, easing closer for the first line only as far as everyone still fits (on a phone in portrait, and at 1280×800, barely at all). It is one smooth camera from the gate to the goodbye: it widens to take in where the neighbours will stand while they are still out of sight, so they walk into the picture, and moves at most 2.5% of its distance in a frame (under 1% while they talk and you stand still; if you walk along the veranda mid-line it widens faster so nobody leaves the screen); after Goodnight it comes back to you as gently, with no jump when the usual camera takes over. The neighbours turn smoothly, over about a third of a second, when the speaker changes and when they set off. An unfinished first evening no longer holds it back. Before six, Today offers “Wait on the veranda until six”, and Sleep asks once before the neighbours have been. If nobody is home, Today and the marker point there; if you walk away while they talk, it ends after three seconds; either way it comes again the next day. While they arrive and talk, everyone else keeps clear of the group and the camera’s foreground (street walkers turn back; the keke and residents on their rounds wait at the edge), and from the moment they set off from the gate until they have gone, a street sign such as Suya that would sit over them, you or your companion fades out over a third of a second, and fades back in once they have gone. After Goodnight they walk off along the street away from you (west, unless a guest stands east of you), so nobody passes between the camera and you, and each goes back to the usual round as soon as no view can see them, joining it where nobody is looking. The evening reminder toast goes as soon as a journal page opens, since Today’s evening card says the same.
- **Tomorrow, and a rating.** After Sleep the night page names what is waiting tomorrow (“Tunde said he would leave something in your pot.”), with one clause about today (Ada approves of where the armchair went, once she has said so), then asks “How was today?” with three faces. After the third day’s rating come two optional questions: one thing you want for your flat and one thing you know about a neighbour.
- **Neighbours who notice.** About forty lines each for Tunde, Mama Nkechi and Ada, chosen from what you have actually done, with no repeats within a day. The lines react to a request rather than repeat its card, the older gift lines no longer promise a housewarming that has already happened, and Ada thanks you for the shells, not the armchair.
- **Sound.** Five short cues (cast, nibble, bite, catch, water), each with something to see as well, because phones are often muted. The Menu has a “Sound: on” / “Sound: off” button, kept per device.
- **A day log for testers.** Each day records when the player first acted, when they slept, the time played with and without panels (counting only while the tab is visible and there was input in the last minute), the rating, the two answers and the day’s events. An undone paint is logged too, a finger held on the touch stick counts as input, and a reload before the first input adds nothing ahead of the day’s wake. Each return is logged with its wall time: Continue, and also the first key, tap or held drag after ten minutes or more with none (or the page shown again after ten minutes hidden), so a tab left open on the morning page still shows when the tester came back; the gates tool takes day three’s start from the first return after day two’s Sleep. The Menu’s copy button, “Copy timeline and days”, copies the first-evening timeline and all the days, followed by a data block for the reviewer’s gates tool (see *Testing with players*).
- No line in the game names a day of the week any more; the HUD calendar is unchanged. The directions chip measures from you (not the companion) and hides during the veranda evening, and the night page no longer doubles the full stop after the home vibe.

Online (two devices): purchases, painting, the crop and the morning card are shared as before. There is no day log, rating or veranda evening, and the new journal and Menu actions (Sound and Camera included) stay on the device that pressed them. The TV glow and the tray follow the shared household (time, party and pieces).

Each module also has its own harness (`verify-tune-v35`, `verify-lines-v35`, `verify-log-v35`, `verify-evening-v35`, `verify-audio-v35`, `verify-lens-v35`, `verify-homeuse-v35`), and `verify-gates-v35` checks the tester gates tool. The live link deploys `master`, so v35 must be merged before testers start.

Open **Tasks** (or **Menu**) for the field journal: Today, Almanac, Garden, Finds, People and Home.

## v34a — Feel the actions

Everything in this slice lives in `dist/src/feel-v34.js`, loaded last; the base page changed only for the script tag and the move-in hint.

- **Carrying the sofa.** Neither holder nor the sofa may step up onto raised ground that is not a veranda slab (the back stairs, the landing, the roof): a step that would raise the level is cancelled like a wall, while a step that keeps or lowers the level is always allowed, so a pair that starts on the lower ramp simply walks down. One rate-limited toast explains that the sofa goes through the narrow passage on the left of the courtyard. The reference level for a carried sofa is the highest of its own resting samples and the two holders' current levels, so a grab from the upper grab window on the ramp still walks down at once. A sofa that an older save left resting on the stairs or the landing slides back (with a toast) to a validated courtyard spot when the world is entered or the sofa is next grabbed: the delivery spot by the gate first, then two courtyard spots, then a short search of nearby ground points, and a spot is taken only when it is collision-free (walls, furniture, the crate), on level ground, a player's width clear of the stair corridor and empty of players. The stair foot itself is never used, since a sofa there would seal the corridor for anyone coming down the ramp. In companion mode the companion carries its end: it follows a breadcrumb trail of the human's path one sofa length behind, pivots round corners by following the path, backs straight up when the human walks into it, swings to a clear angle at tight doorways, and lets go when the human sets the sofa down. The human holder walks at 0.92× instead of 0.7× in companion mode so the pair keeps at least 90 percent of solo walking speed on open ground along the sofa (a diagonal push starts nearer 87 percent while the sofa swings round). Two-human carrying keeps the base symmetric maths apart from the stairs rule. The move-in hints point players left to the passage and away from the stairs.
- **A first evening with a companion.** During the guided shop stage the companion shops for jollof ingredients only; its bag counts as the household's and is unpacked when the human is in the flat; a companion that is far from the kitchen is called in over a ground-level route before the stations open (not while it is up on the roof or the roof sequence is on: then the usual refusal stands), and a toast says when it has arrived so the stove can be used. The party picks, cooking station, gift and tomorrow choice already worked alone and are now proven in the harness with the companion driven by `aiDrive`/`aiThink`.
- **Stage timing log.** `S.firstEvening.log` records one entry per stage transition (`stage`, `at` wall-clock ms, `played`, `day`, game `time`). `played` counts seconds with the tab visible, panels included (the shop, the kitchen and the party are part of the evening); nothing counts while the tab is hidden. A save from before v34 gets one seeded entry for its current stage (played 0) on the first reconcile. The log persists in saves and is kept for local and companion households only: in online play (`NET.on`) nothing is seeded, counted or shown, and it is stripped from the shared household document, so it is absent there. The two Menu actions (`copyTimeline`, `menubtn`) are local-only and never replay on the other peer. The Menu shows it as its own line directly above the actions (*First evening: move 0 min · shop 0.4 min · total 3.2 min*), and **Copy evening timeline**, beside the tasks button, puts a compact text summary on the clipboard (or shows it in a panel when the clipboard is unavailable). This is the measurement for the four-of-five testers gate.
- **Phone floor.** When play (not panels, not the first 15 s after boot) stays under 24 fps for ten contiguous seconds the render scale steps down one notch (1.6 → 1.25 → 1.0); another ten seconds under 24 fps at 1.0 switches to lighter graphics live (shadows off and 1024 map, half the clouds and walkers, lite water, no rim light) and stores `lite` so the next load starts lighter. One toast per real step: a notch that would not change the pixel ratio (a device already at 1.0) is taken silently and the next notch follows at once, so such a device reaches lighter graphics after the first ten-second window, and a page already in lighter graphics gets no lite toast. The Menu shows the current state; it never steps up by itself. `perfDecide34` is the decision function the harness exercises.
- **Loading.** All sixteen wardrobe pieces are requested at once, the players' own pieces first (from the saved household or the creator defaults), with the per-piece callbacks and the single `spawnNPCs` preserved, so the creator preview appears as soon as the current look's pieces are in rather than after all sixteen. Measured under software rendering: 'Preparing your player' cleared 14.7 s after the creator opened instead of 66.3 s, and all sixteen pieces were cached at 91 s instead of 233 s.

## Run a local copy

The game needs no build step or package installation. Serve `dist` through HTTP:

```sh
python -m http.server 8000 --directory dist
```

Open `http://localhost:8000`. Opening `index.html` through `file://` can block asset loading.
